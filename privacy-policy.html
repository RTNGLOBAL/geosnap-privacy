GeoSnap Privacy Policy

Effective date: 31 October 2026 · Package com.rtnglobal.geosnap · Published by [DEVELOPER / PUBLISHER NAME]

In short: GeoSnap does not collect, store or share any of your data on any server. The developer operates no server, and the app has no advertising, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking code, no account system and no internet permission.

1. Location

  • GeoSnap asks for location permission so it can print your location on photos and write it to the photo's metadata. Precise or approximate access is your choice, and the app works without it.
  • Location is read from your phone's own location system (GPS/GNSS and, where available, the network location provider) only while the camera screen is open. GeoSnap never uses location in the background and does not request background-location permission.
  • GeoSnap does not keep a location history. The current position is held in memory while the camera is open and is saved only inside photos you take.
  • If you turn on Compass direction or Level, GeoSnap reads the phone's motion sensors (compass, accelerometer) while the camera is open. These readings need no permission, are not stored, and only the camera direction at the moment you take a photo is kept, in that photo.

2. Place names (reverse geocoding)

To show a place name such as "Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan", GeoSnap uses Android's built-in Geocoder service. GeoSnap passes the coordinates of your current position to that service on your device. On many devices this service is provided by the operating system or device manufacturer (for example by Google Play services) and may look up the coordinates online. That processing is performed by the provider of your device's geocoder under its own privacy policy (for Google: policies.google.com/privacy). GeoSnap itself has no internet permission and does not send the coordinates anywhere.

Recent place names are cached in memory only, so the same lookup isn't repeated, and are discarded when the app closes. You can stop place-name lookups at any time by turning off Settings → Location name. GPS coordinates and the stamp work fully offline.

3. Photos and metadata

  • Photos are saved only on your device: in Pictures/GeoSnap (shared photo storage), or, if you turn off "Save to gallery", in GeoSnap's private app storage, where they are removed if you uninstall the app.
  • Each photo contains the visible stamp you configured (any of: your note, place name, coordinates, camera direction, altitude, date and time) and EXIF metadata: GPS latitude, longitude, altitude, accuracy and camera direction (when known), your note (when it uses only basic Latin characters), capture date and time, and basic camera details reported by your phone.
  • The note you type in Settings is stored only on your device, in the app's settings, and is printed on the photos you take.
  • GeoSnap has no cloud storage and never uploads photos.

4. Sharing

When you tap Share, you choose how photos are shared, and they are handed to the app you pick through Android's standard share sheet:

  • Share with location: the photo is shared as saved, with its printed stamp and GPS metadata, so the recipient can see where it was taken.
  • Share without GPS data: GeoSnap makes a temporary copy with all GPS metadata removed and shares that copy. The stamp printed on the photo is part of the image and stays visible. Temporary copies are kept in the app's private cache and removed automatically once they are a day old, the next time GeoSnap is opened or shares a photo.

What happens next is governed by the app you shared with.

7. Children

GeoSnap is a general-purpose camera app and is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

8. Settings storage

Your watermark and app preferences are stored locally on your device using Android DataStore and are excluded from cloud backup. Uninstalling the app removes them.
